Welcome back to the weekly ritual where we throw two unrelated economic time series into a blender and act shocked at what comes out. This week's contestants included discontinued hospital output indexes, Long Island house prices, government grants, mining price deflators, Canadian recessions, and San Francisco's tech vibes. A truly cohesive lineup that no sane research agenda would ever assemble on purpose. As always: these are randomly paired series, nobody is claiming anything causes anything, and spurious correlation isn't the bug here, it's the whole point.

Monday (August 18) gave us the classic trend mirage in its purest form. Hospital and nursing facility output versus Nassau-Suffolk house prices came in with a gloriously "significant" raw result, an R-squared near 0.60 and a p-value with enough zeros to make you gasp. Truly, at last, proof that hospitals and Long Island real estate are locked in cosmic harmony. Then we detrended it and the whole thing evaporated: R-squared collapsed to 0.08 and the p-value ballooned to 0.23, which is statistics-speak for "never mind." Turns out both series just went up over time, like nearly everything does. Case dismissed.

Tuesday (August 19) barely bothered showing up. U.S. government grants versus a mining sector price deflator managed a limp raw p-value of 0.08 -- not even significant if you're being strict, and I am -- and once detrended it slumped to a magnificent 0.53. That's the numerical equivalent of a shrug. Nothing here, no trend, no relationship, not even a fun fake one. We salute its honesty and move on.

Then Wednesday (August 20) actually delivered the rare thing this project pretends to be looking for. Canadian recession indicators versus the San Francisco Tech Pulse held up in both the raw and detrended versions, with a negative relationship staying significant after we stripped the trends out (p-value still hovering around one-in-fifty-billion territory). The R-squared is small, so calm down, but directionally it's the kind of thing that even makes a little sense: when Canada's in recession, the broader environment tech thrives in tends to sag too. Do I believe there's a deep structural bond between Ottawa's business cycle and the Bay Area's gadget mood? Absolutely not, and neither should you -- but as far as randomly paired discontinued FRED series go, this one earned its gold star. See you next week for more nonsense.
