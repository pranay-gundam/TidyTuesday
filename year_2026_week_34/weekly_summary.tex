This week the machine reached into the great economic tombola and pulled out a truly inspired duo: a discontinued index tracking the real output of American hospitals and nursing homes, and the house price index for Nassau and Suffolk counties on Long Island. Because nothing says "meaningful economic inquiry" like asking whether the fortunes of Long Island real estate can be explained by how many people are checking into New York-adjacent nursing facilities. Naturally, we ran the numbers with the appropriate gravitas.

And oh, the raw regression delivered. An R-squared of about 0.60, a p-value with more zeros after the decimal than I have fingers, coefficients standing tall and statistically proud. For one shining moment it looked like we had cracked it: hospital output and Long Island home values, locked in an eternal cosmic dance. Somebody call an economics journal. Somebody call a realtor. Somebody call whoever discontinued this series in 2016 and tell them what they gave up on.

Then, as always, the detrended version wandered in and turned off the lights. Strip out the fact that both series mostly just drift upward over the years -- as basically every economic quantity in a growing economy tends to do -- and the whole thing evaporates. R-squared collapses to a limp 0.08, the p-value balloons to a deeply unimpressed 0.23, and the coefficient's confidence interval sprawls comfortably across zero like it's on vacation. In other words: the "relationship" was just two things going up at the same time for entirely unrelated reasons. A textbook trend mirage, and exactly the kind of thing this project exists to point at and laugh.

As always, the standard disclaimer applies with feeling: these are randomly paired series with zero theoretical reason to be connected, and any correlation here is a happy accident of arithmetic, not evidence that hospital beds move mortgage prices. Spurious is the house style. This week the raw regression flexed hard and the detrended version quietly explained that it was all a misunderstanding -- which is honestly the most predictable plot twist we've got.
