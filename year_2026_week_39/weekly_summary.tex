Welcome back to the weekly parade of numbers that have no business being in the same room together, let alone the same scatterplot. This week our little algorithm reached into the FRED grab bag and pulled out New England electricity prices, house listings in Flathead County Montana, wholesale payrolls, some discontinued bank asset ratio, and — my personal favorite — the number of tax exemptions claimed by people under 65 in New Mexico. As always, none of these things asked to be compared to each other. We did it anyway.

Monday kicked us off with the textbook trend mirage. New England commercial electricity prices versus a medical services price index posted a jaw-dropping R-squared of 0.93 in the raw regression, which, if you didn't know better, would have you drafting a Nobel acceptance speech. Then we detrended it and the R-squared cratered to a heroic 0.02 with a p-value of 0.51, which in technical terms means "absolutely nothing is happening here." Two things went up over two decades, as prices tend to do, and the raw regression got very excited about it. Case closed, mirage confirmed, everyone go home.

But then the week got genuinely weird, in that some of these actually held up. Tuesday's pairing of Flathead County active home listings with U.S. imports of maintenance and repair services survived detrending — it actually got a bit stronger, jumping to a still-modest R-squared around 0.19 with a p-value in the "huh, that's real" range. Wednesday was the standout: wholesale trade payrolls versus the Dover, Delaware house price index kept a chunky R-squared of 0.73 even after stripping out the shared trend, which is the kind of result that makes me squint suspiciously at my own code. Thursday's Southwest central-bank GDP versus that discontinued Pacific bank ratio also clung to significance detrended (R-squared crept up to 0.28, and negative, no less), and Friday's education-and-communication CPI versus New Mexico tax exemptions doubled its R-squared to a frankly show-offy 0.51 once detrended. Four out of five survivors is an unusually strong week for a project whose entire premise is finding nonsense.

Now, before anyone starts building a hedge fund around Delaware home prices and wholesale payroll indexes, let me remind you with all the deadpan sincerity I can muster: these are randomly paired series. I did not have a theory. There is no theory. Nobody is claiming that New Mexico tax filers are secretly driving the price of your kid's textbooks, and nobody sane would. A relationship "holding up under detrending" makes it more interesting than the electricity-price fiasco, not actually meaningful — it just means the coincidence is a little more committed to the bit. Spurious correlation is the house specialty here, served fresh daily, and this week's suspiciously robust survivors are best enjoyed the way you'd enjoy a horoscope that happened to be technically accurate. See you next week for more statistically confident gibberish.
