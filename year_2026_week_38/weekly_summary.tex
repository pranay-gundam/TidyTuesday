Welcome back to the show where we throw two economic strangers into a room, make them regress against each other, and act shocked when they have chemistry. This week's blind date was a real barn-burner: the All-Transactions House Price Index for Oxnard-Thousand Oaks-Ventura, California, meets Financial Business Gross Fixed Investment in Nonresidential Equipment, Software, and Structures -- a series so beloved it scores a popularity of 1 out of a possible universe, and which was so committed to the bit that it got itself discontinued in 2017. Truly the kind of matchup you dream about.

And the raw regression? Oh, be still my beating heart. An R-squared of 0.87. An F-statistic in the four digits. A p-value with so many zeros after the decimal point I briefly wondered if my keyboard was stuck. If you took this number at face value, you'd conclude that Ventura County home prices and the equipment-buying habits of financial firms are practically the same phenomenon wearing two different hats. Case closed, Nobel prize incoming, someone call the newspapers.

Except, of course, this is exactly the trap this whole project exists to faceplant into. Both of these series are long-lived numbers that mostly go up over the decades, and when you make two things-that-go-up sit next to each other, they will hold hands regardless of whether they've ever actually met. So we stripped out each series' own march-of-time trend and re-ran it. The R-squared face-planted from a dizzying 0.87 all the way down to 0.23, and the constant term collapsed to essentially zero with a p-value of 0.9999999999 -- which is statistics-speak for "yeah, that was mostly the trend, buddy." There's still a faint whiff of a leftover relationship in the detrended version (the slope stayed significant, so I won't pretend it's completely dead), but it's a shadow of the grand romance the raw numbers were selling.

As always, the mandatory disclaimer, which I will keep repeating until I'm blue in the face: these are randomly paired series with no causal story, no theoretical grounding, and absolutely nothing rigorous behind them. Spurious correlation isn't the exception here -- it's the house specialty, the whole menu, the reason we get out of bed. This week's headliner was a textbook trend-mirage that mostly evaporated once we took away its shared drift, and that's precisely the kind of dunk this project lives for.
