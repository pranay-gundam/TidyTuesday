Welcome back to the only newsletter brave enough to shove two completely unrelated economic time series into a blender and describe what comes out as ``research.'' This week the random number gods handed us a truly inspired pairing: Coinbase Bitcoin, the digital asset that has personally ruined several dinner conversations, versus the San Francisco Tech Pulse index -- which, and I cannot stress this enough, has been DISCONTINUED since March 2020. So yes, we are correlating a live cryptocurrency against a series that has been resting in peace for over six years. Naturally, this is where science happens.

The raw regression came out swinging with an R-squared of about 0.70, which in the language of overexcited spreadsheet enthusiasts means ``oh my god, we found something.'' Seventy percent! A p-value with more zeros than a crypto whale's wallet! If you stopped reading here, you might conclude that Bitcoin's price and a defunct San Francisco tech vibe-o-meter are practically soulmates. And you would be gloriously, hilariously wrong, because this is exactly the kind of trend-mirage this whole project exists to point and laugh at: two things that both drifted upward for years will happily hold hands in a raw regression whether or not they've ever actually met.

Enter the detrended version, the great humbler. Once we strip out each series' own long-run drift, that majestic 0.70 R-squared faceplants down to a deeply humble 0.14. The relationship doesn't vanish entirely -- it's technically still ``significant'' at the p-around-0.003 level, which I suppose earns it a participation trophy -- but going from explaining 70 percent of the wiggle to explaining 14 percent is the statistical equivalent of a superhero who loses his powers the moment the cameras show up. So most of that dazzling raw fit was just two lines going up together and taking credit for teamwork.

As always, the mandatory reminder for anyone tempted to build a trading strategy or a thesis around this: these are RANDOMLY paired series, there is no causal story, no rigorous claim, and absolutely no reason Bitcoin should care about a discontinued Bay Area index that stopped updating around the time we all learned to bake sourdough. Spurious correlation isn't the bug here, it's the entire premise. Tune in next week when we heroically torture two more innocent datasets that never asked to be in the same graph.
