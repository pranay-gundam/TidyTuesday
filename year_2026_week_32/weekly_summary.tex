Welcome back to the only newsletter brave enough to shackle together economic data that has never met, never will meet, and frankly wants nothing to do with each other. This week the random-number gods really outdid themselves in pairing series that share nothing but the misfortune of living in the same database. As always, the house rules apply: everything you're about to read is a coincidence dressed up in a lab coat, and any conclusion you draw is legally your problem, not mine.

Monday kicked off with the headline act: Coinbase Bitcoin versus the San Francisco Tech Pulse, a tech-vibes index so discontinued it stopped observing reality in 2020. The raw regression clocked in at a swaggering R-squared of 0.70 with a p-value that has more zeros after the decimal than I have will to count them. "Bitcoin explains tech!" screamed the graph, before I remembered that both things went up over time, which is the statistical equivalent of noticing that two people standing on the same escalator are "rising together." And sure enough, detrend it and the R-squared face-plants down to 0.14. Now, mildly interesting: it's still technically significant after detrending (p around 0.003), so there's a faint, wobbly pulse of a real relationship here rather than pure escalator magic. I'm not going to build a hedge fund on it, but for this project that counts as a genuinely tantalizing morsel. Don't get excited. Okay, get a little excited. Then stop.

Tuesday and the rest of the week were where the dreams went to die, in the most soothing way possible. The Philadelphia Fed's capital-expenditures diffusion index against a real user cost index of some money aggregate produced an R-squared of 0.0006 and a p-value of 0.89, which is math's polite way of saying "these two have never once thought about each other." Wednesday paired Italy's central-bank foreign-exchange interventions from the 1990s with U.S. real-estate loan delinquency rates, and the raw regression flashed a "significant" p-value of 0.04 like it had something to tell us — until detrending mercilessly strangled it back to a p-value of 0.35. Textbook trend mirage; a shimmering oasis that turns to sand the moment you remove the shared drift. And Thursday's grand finale, a NAFTA recession on/off switch versus the U.S. current account balance, managed R-squared values of 0.005 raw and 0.0004 detrended, numbers so close to zero they should qualify for emotional support.

So the scoreboard for the week: one relationship that survived detrending with a heartbeat (Bitcoin and the ghost of San Francisco tech), and three that collapsed into the void the instant we asked them to prove they weren't just riding the same trend. This is, statistically speaking, a completely normal week for us — spurious correlation isn't the bug, it's the entire genre. None of this implies causation, none of it implies a real link, and if it did, the universe would owe us an apology.
