Welcome back to the weekly parade of numbers we have no business putting in the same room together. This week our tireless little robot reached into the FRED grab-bag five times and pulled out such crowd-pleasers as New Zealand's pandemic anxiety, American telecom construction spending, several flavors of consumer price index, discontinued bank loan rates, and — a personal favorite — the price of having a diseased circulatory system. Truly the stuff of Nobel dreams. As always, these are random pairings, so please holster any urge to build a theory of the universe out of them.

Monday kicked things off with a spectacular nothingburger: World Pandemic Uncertainty Index for New Zealand versus U.S. communication construction spending. The raw regression coughed up an R-squared of 0.008 and a p-value of 0.32, which is statistics-speak for "no, and please stop asking." The detrended version was, if anything, even more of a shrug. A refreshingly honest start — sometimes two series really do have nothing to say to each other, and I respect that.

Then Tuesday showed up and stole the entire week. Northeast core CPI versus U.S. transportation-services CPI posted a raw R-squared of 0.97, which is the kind of number that makes you nervously check whether you accidentally regressed a series against itself. But here's the shocker: it actually survived detrending, holding an R-squared of 0.84 and a p-value with twenty-three zeros in front of it. Fold me up and call me stationary — this is the rare one that holds up in both. Of course, "two different measures of consumer prices tend to move together" is roughly as earth-shattering as discovering that both my socks got wet in the rain, but by the delirious standards of this project it's a genuine standout. Wednesday's medical-services-price pairing pulled the same trick in reverse: a gorgeous 0.98 raw R-squared that vaporized to essentially zero once the shared trend was stripped out. That, dear reader, is the trend mirage in its natural habitat — two things drifting upward together, holding hands, meaning absolutely nothing.

Thursday gave us government employment-training benefits versus a heroically discontinued bank loan rate, where the raw regression found nothing (R-squared 0.004) but the detrended version squeaked out a "significant" p-value of 0.038 with a whopping 22\% of variance explained — the statistical equivalent of a participation trophy, and one I'd gently ignore. Friday closed us out with Great Lakes financial-services spending versus circulatory-disease prices: a moderately impressive raw fit that, once again, evaporated after detrending. So the final tally is one relationship that survived (two price indexes being price indexes at each other) and a whole bunch of shared-drift illusions doing exactly what they always do. Remember, spurious is the house special here — we're not proving anything, we're just enjoying the show. See you next week for more consenting-adult data we forced into a scatterplot.
