Welcome back to another thrilling week of Randomly Yoking Two Economic Time Series Together And Praying, the world's most rigorous scientific enterprise, if by "rigorous" you mean "a dice roll wearing a lab coat." As always, the usual disclaimer applies before anyone drafts a policy memo off this: these pairs are chosen by a machine with no taste, no agenda, and no idea what any of these acronyms mean. Spurious is the house style here. If something looks meaningful, that's the correlation talking, and it lies.

We opened Sunday on a real barnburner: the World Pandemic Uncertainty Index for New Zealand versus Total Private Construction Spending on Communication in the United States. I know, I know, try to contain yourselves. This one did us the enormous favor of being useless right out of the gate -- an R-squared of 0.008 and a p-value of 0.32, meaning even the raw regression, the one that will grab any excuse to look impressed, just shrugged. Detrended, it got even sadder. A rare case of a pairing so unrelated that not even our trend-hungry method could fake a spark. Refreshingly honest failure. Ten out of ten, no notes.

Then came the two showstoppers, and this is where you have to keep your wits about you. Monday paired a Northeast core CPI series with U.S. transportation-services CPI and posted a raw R-squared of 0.97, which is the kind of number that makes people whisper "Nobel." But here's the twist that makes this week actually interesting: it held up detrended too, still wildly significant with an R-squared around 0.84. That's not the usual trend mirage collapsing under scrutiny -- that's two inflation measures genuinely moving together even after you strip the shared upward march out, which, given that both are literally chunks of the same consumer price index, is about as close to "well, obviously" as this project ever gets. Tuesday's pairing of an "Other Goods and Services" CPI against a Medical Services price index pulled the exact opposite stunt: a gorgeous 0.98 raw R-squared that evaporated into a 0.00005 nothingburger the second we detrended it. Classic. Two things that both went up over the years, holding hands purely because time passed. Chef's kiss of spuriousness.

The back half of the week was mostly a parade of the mildly desperate. Government employment-and-training benefits versus a discontinued bank loan rate found basically nothing raw, then coughed up a barely-significant detrended p-value of 0.038 with an R-squared of 0.22 -- the statistical equivalent of a polite golf clap. Great Lakes financial-services spending versus a circulatory-disease price index looked decent raw and then folded detrended, another trend impostor unmasked. And we closed Saturday on Middle Atlantic residential natural gas prices versus the San Francisco Tech Pulse, which squeaked in at p=0.046 detrended with an R-squared of 0.04, i.e., technically significant and practically a rounding error. So to recap: one genuinely believable relationship (that was two flavors of CPI, so, cheating), a couple of textbook trend mirages, and a handful of coin-flips pretending to matter. Do not build anything on any of this. See you next week for more.
