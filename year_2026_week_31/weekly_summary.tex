Welcome back, dear reader, to the only weekly newsletter brave enough to shove two economic time series into a blender and describe the resulting smoothie as "science." As always, the machine picked these pairings at random, meaning any relationship you see below has roughly the same epistemic standing as a horoscope. Let us proceed with the appropriate mixture of enthusiasm and shame.

Monday, July 27, gave us the crossover episode nobody demanded: the World Pandemic Uncertainty Index for New Zealand versus U.S. private construction spending on communication infrastructure. The result? Absolutely nothing. An R-squared of 0.008, which is statistics-speak for "these two series have never met and would not recognize each other at a party." The p-value strolled in at 0.32, the detrended version somehow did even worse, and I, for one, am relieved. New Zealand's pandemic anxieties are not, it turns out, secretly wiring up America's phone lines. Sleep easy.

Then came the drama. Tuesday, July 28, paired a Northeast urban core-inflation index against the CPI for transportation services, and the raw regression posted an R-squared of 0.97 with a p-value so small it needs scientific notation to fit on the page. "Aha," you cry, "a trend mirage, the house special!" And normally you'd be right -- but hold onto your spreadsheets, because when we stripped out the shared time trend, the detrended version still clocked an R-squared of 0.84 and a p-value with twenty-three zeros hiding behind the decimal. This is the rare, genuinely-interesting-looking result: two different price indexes moving together even after we remove the "everything gets more expensive forever" component. Shocking twist -- inflation correlates with inflation. Alert the Nobel committee. This is the closest thing to a real relationship this project has stumbled into in weeks, and it feels deeply weird to admit it.

Wednesday, July 29, tried the same trick and got humbled. "Other Goods and Services" CPI versus a medical services price index looked gorgeous raw (R-squared 0.98!), and then the detrending machine walked in and reduced the relationship to a p-value of 0.97 and an R-squared of 0.00005 -- a collapse so total it's almost artistic. Textbook trend mirage: two things that both go up over time, holding hands only because time exists. Thursday closed us out with government employment-and-training benefits versus a discontinued bank loan rate, which produced nothing raw but a faintly significant negative wiggle once detrended (p around 0.04). I'd love to theorize, but with an R-squared of 0.22 and a series that literally has "DISCONTINUED" in its name, I think the responsible move is to gently pretend it didn't happen. As ever: these are random pairings, no causal claims are being made, and if you take investment advice from this newsletter that is a decision you made entirely on your own.
