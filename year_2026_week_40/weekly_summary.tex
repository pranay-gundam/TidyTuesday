\section{Weekly Summary}

\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=yellow!10,colframe=black!50,title=AI-Generated Content]
\noindent The summary below was written by Claude (Anthropic) from that week's regression outputs and series descriptions. It has not been reviewed or fact-checked by a human, and should be read as an automated, informal recap -- not analysis.

Welcome back to the weekly ritual where we take two economic time series that have never met, sit them down together, and demand they explain their relationship to us. As always, nobody asked for this, and yet here we are. This week featured just two brave contestants, and both delivered performances I frankly wasn't emotionally prepared for.

First up, on September 28th, we paired Real GDP of Wood Product Manufacturing in the Great Lakes region with, and I cannot stress how naturally these two belong together, Mobile Cellular Subscriptions per 100 People in Europe and Central Asia. The raw regression looked at these two and shrugged: an R-squared of about 0.08 and a p-value of 0.14, which is the statistical equivalent of a mumbled "eh, maybe." But then we detrended, stripping out each series' own march through time, and against all reason the relationship got stronger, not weaker. Suddenly R-squared doubled to 0.18 and the p-value slid under 0.05. So the message is: once you remove the boring shared drift, Great Lakes lumber and European cell phones have a modest, statistically respectable negative wiggle-for-wiggle correlation. Do I have a theory? Yes. Is it that Europeans upgrade their phones in years when Michigan mills feel sad? It is not, because there is no theory, because this is nonsense, but a slightly more robust flavor of nonsense than usual.

Then September 29th arrived and produced the kind of raw regression number that makes you want to lie down. Shares of gross domestic income going to employee wages (a series that has been discontinued, God rest it) versus the number of Black or African American people not in the labor force. The raw R-squared was 0.886. Zero point eight eight six! In an honest project we'd frame this and hang it on the wall. But this is not an honest project, this is the Trend Mirage Detection Bureau, and sure enough, once we detrended both series, that gorgeous 0.89 evaporated down to 0.23. A textbook case of two things drifting through the twentieth century in loose lockstep and pretending it means something. That said, 0.23 with a p-value under 0.002 didn't fully collapse, so there's a faint pulse still detectable after the trend transplant. I'd call it "interesting," in the sense that literally everything here is interesting if you squint hard enough and have no standards.

The usual disclaimer, now with feeling: these are randomly paired series, chosen by a dice-rolling script that has no idea what economics is and even less idea what causation means. Spurious correlation isn't the risk here, it's the entire business model. Wood products do not control European phones, and please, for the love of all that is measurable, do not cite any of this in anything. See you next week for more strangers being forced to hold hands.
