Welcome back to the weekly ritual where we throw two economic time series into a blender, hit "regress," and pretend the result means something. This week the machine was feeling a little sleepy, which I frankly appreciate, because it means fewer opportunities to embarrass ourselves in front of the entire discipline of statistics.

First up, on August 31, we paired the thrilling "Percent of Value of Loans, SBA-Backed, Zero Interval, Moderate Risk" (a series so beloved it has a popularity score of 1, and was also, delightfully, discontinued) with house prices in Ft. Lauderdale. And the verdict? Nothing. The raw regression coughed up a p-value of 0.33, which in human terms means "please stop looking at me." The detrended version was equally unbothered. So here we have the rare case of two series that couldn't be induced to correlate even by our aggressively lenient standards. Refreshing, honestly. No trend mirage, no fake romance, just two strangers who made eye contact and immediately walked away.

September 2 is where it got briefly exciting, which is to say I sat up in my chair for approximately four seconds. We matched "Aggregate Weekly Hours in Durable Goods Manufacturing" against "Demand Deposits Due to Foreign Official Institutions" (also discontinued, because this project apparently only invites series that have already given up on life). The raw regression came in significant -- p-value of 0.006, a real "ooh" moment -- suggesting that as foreign officials parked money in banks, factory workers... clocked slightly fewer hours? A thrilling geopolitical thriller of a correlation. And then we detrended it, and the whole thing evaporated like a puddle in a Florida parking lot. P-value shot up to 0.82, R-squared plummeted to a heroic 0.0003. Classic. Both series just happened to drift over the decades, the raw regression mistook that shared drift for love, and detrending mercifully filed for annulment.

So the scoreboard this week: one big fat nothing and one textbook trend-mirage that fooled absolutely no one who bothered to look twice. As always, I remind you that these are randomly paired series with zero causal story, zero rigorous claim, and zero reason to exist beyond my own amusement. Spurious correlation isn't the bug here -- it's the entire feature. See you next week, when the blender will inevitably find two discontinued series drifting upward together and try to convince us they're soulmates.
