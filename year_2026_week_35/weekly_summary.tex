Welcome back to the weekly digest of Randomly Yelling Numbers At Each Other, the only research program where the hypotheses are made up and the correlations don't matter. This week our tireless robot pulled two pairs of series out of the FRED grab bag, and I regret to inform you that neither of them is going to make us rich.

Monday-ish gave us the crown jewel: "Other Business Receivables Owned by Finance Companies" versus "Diffusion Indexes, 3-Month Span: Total Nonfarm." Two phrases that, spoken aloud at a party, would clear the room in record time. And the result? An R-squared of about 0.0065. Sit down for this. That means we explained a heart-stopping 0.65 percent of the variation, leaving a mere 99.35 percent to the imagination. The p-value clocked in around 0.096, which is that special zone where a desperate grad student squints and mutters "marginally significant" while their advisor slowly leaves the building. The best part: after detrending, absolutely nothing changed -- still weak, still not significant, still basically a flat line pretending to have a personality. So there wasn't even a trend-mirage to expose here. It's just genuinely, honestly, sincerely nothing. Refreshing, really.

Tuesday's contestant was even more spectacular, in the sense that it did not happen at all. We matched "Regular Seasonal Factors: Auto Production" against "U.S. Granted Patents: Reissue Patents Originating in Romania" -- yes, Romania, reissue patents specifically, a data series with a popularity score of 1, meaning the only person who ever loved it was our robot. The regression returned a cascade of zeros and NaNs, which is statistics-speak for "I could not even." One series is monthly seasonal wiggles through 2027, the other is a trickle of annual Romanian patents ending in 2020, and when they tried to overlap they produced the numerical equivalent of two people waving at each other from different time zones. No R-squared, no F-statistic, no closure. Just void.

As always, the mandatory reminder: these pairs are chosen by a dice roll, not a theory. There is no causal claim, no rigorous inference, and no reason on Earth that Romanian reissue patents should have anything to do with auto seasonal factors. Spurious is the house style; a real relationship would frankly be a scandal. This week the numbers behaved themselves and refused to correlate, which is either deeply boring or a small victory for scientific integrity. See you next week, when we once again ask two unrelated strangers to slow dance and act surprised when they step on each other's feet.
