Welcome back to the weekly ritual where we feed the slot machine of American economic data two randomly chosen series and pray to the gods of statistics that something, anything, happens. This week was a short one -- just two days of pairings -- and I regret to inform you that the universe remained stubbornly, magnificently indifferent to our efforts. But let's walk through the wreckage together, because that's what we do here.

Monday the 19th gave us a true clash of titans: "Weekday-Basis Seasonal Factors: Light Truck Production" versus "Export Price Index for Nonferrous Metal (Except Aluminum) Production and Processing." I was on the edge of my seat. And then the numbers came in with a p-value of 0.93 and an R-squared of 0.0000349, which is a very fancy way of saying these two series have never met, will never meet, and would not recognize each other at a party. The raw regression found nothing. The detrended regression found even more nothing, if that's possible. For once, both versions agree completely: there is no relationship here, there was never going to be a relationship here, and the seasonal quirks of light truck assembly lines have absolutely no opinion on the export price of non-aluminum metals. Refreshing, honestly. No mirage to debunk because there was no oasis to begin with.

Tuesday the 20th is where it got spicy, and by spicy I mean textbook. We paired the Import Price Index for Latin American Agriculture, Forestry, Fishing and Hunting against "Checkable Deposits and Currency Held by the 50th to 90th Wealth Percentiles" -- a series so dead it literally has "DISCONTINUED" in the title, which is the data equivalent of regressing on a ghost. The raw regression lit up like a Christmas tree: p-value of 0.0006, R-squared of 0.27, the whole dramatic production. For one brief, shining moment, you might have believed that how much cash the upper-middle class keeps in their checking accounts is deeply entwined with the price of imported Latin American produce. Then we detrended it, and the relationship evaporated so fast it left a little puff of smoke -- p-value rocketed up to 0.86 and the R-squared cratered to basically zero. This, dear reader, is the exact trend-mirage this project exists to mock: two series that happened to drift the same way over the years, holding hands only because time was dragging them both along in the same direction.

So the official tally this week is: one pair that found nothing and admitted it, and one pair that pretended to find something and immediately recanted under mild questioning. No genuine relationships survived both regressions, which is roughly what you should expect, because -- and I cannot stress this enough -- we are pulling random pairs of series out of a giant database and shoving them into a straight line. There is no theory here, no causal story, no hypothesis beyond "huh, I wonder." Spurious correlation is not a bug in this project; it is the entire personality of this project. Tune in next week, when we will once again confuse coincidence for meaning and then bravely talk ourselves out of it.
