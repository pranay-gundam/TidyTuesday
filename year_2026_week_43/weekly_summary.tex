Welcome back to another thrilling installment of "What Happens When You Throw Two Economic Series In A Blender And Hit Go." This week featured exactly one matchup, and honestly, after seeing the results, I understand why the universe decided one was enough. In the red corner, we have the Weekday-Basis Seasonal Factors for Light Truck Production -- a series so niche and so beloved that FRED rates its popularity at a cool "1." In the blue corner, the Export Price Index for Nonferrous Metal (Except Aluminum, because aluminum, as always, gets its own table at the party). Also popularity 1. Truly a clash of titans that no one asked for.

The raw regression came back with a p-value of 0.926, which is statistics-speak for "please stop looking at me." The R-squared clocked in at roughly 0.000035, meaning the light truck seasonal factor explains approximately zero percent of the metal export prices, give or take zero percent. For a project that lives and breathes off spurious correlations propped up by shared trends, this is almost disappointing -- we didn't even get a trend mirage to dunk on. The two series couldn't be bothered to drift in the same direction together, let alone pretend to have a relationship.

And then, as if to twist the knife, the detrended version somehow did even less. P-value of 0.886, R-squared of 0.00008, and a slope so flat you could use it as a spirit level. Normally the detrended regression is where we catch raw "hits" red-handed as frauds, but there was nothing to catch here -- this relationship was dead on arrival in both rounds, which, I'll grudgingly admit, makes it unusually consistent. Zero relationship raw, zero relationship detrended. At least it's honest.

As always, the obligatory reminder: these series were paired by a random number generator with no judgment, taste, or shame, and absolutely no causal or rigorous statistical claim is being made. Spurious correlation is the house specialty around here; a genuine nothingburger like this week's is actually the rare refreshing palate cleanser. Light truck seasonal factors do not move nonferrous metal export prices, nobody thought they did, and now we have a p-value of 0.93 to not prove it. See you next week for more of the same.
