Welcome back, fellow connoisseurs of statistical nonsense. This week our little random-pair machine dragged a truly deranged cast of characters out of the FRED basement: a Danish green-energy stock index, a discontinued Polish recession indicator, the export price of car electronics, Chilean purchases of U.S. agency bonds, auto leases, mortgage-backed securities, covered employment, house prices in multiple census divisions, child poverty in Leon County Florida, and some employment cost indices for good measure. As always, none of these things have any earthly reason to be in the same regression together, which is of course the entire point.

Monday gave us the OMX Copenhagen Alternative Energy Price Index versus Poland's recession indicator, and I am thrilled to report an R-squared of a blistering 0.06, which is to say the model explains approximately nothing. The detrended version actually crept up to 0.07, so if you squint and ignore everything about statistics, Danish solar stocks and Polish recessions have a faint, vanishingly weak, barely-there relationship that survives detrending. Hold your applause. Tuesday was the real comedy, though: car-electronics export prices versus Chilean bond transactions produced a p-value of 0.43 raw and 0.90 detrended, which in technical terms means "lol, no." Two series walked into a regression and left as total strangers. Beautiful.

Now for the trend mirages, which this project adores. Wednesday's auto-leases-versus-foreign-mortgage-securities pairing posted a jaw-dropping raw R-squared of 0.81 -- gasp! clutch pearls! -- and while it did survive detrending at a still-respectable 0.45, let's all remember both of these are dollar-denominated financial piles that mostly just grow over time, so "they go up together" is hardly front-page material. Thursday's covered employment versus East North Central house prices was the classic bait-and-switch: a magnificent 0.79 raw R-squared that face-planted to 0.17 once the shared upward drift got stripped out. Friday was even more damning -- Leon County child poverty versus San Francisco high-tier home prices looked meaningful raw (R-squared 0.42) and then utterly evaporated to 0.006 when detrended, a p-value of 0.71 practically shrugging in your face. And Saturday's Mid-Atlantic employment versus the compensation cost index pulled the same trick: a gorgeous 0.77 raw that collapsed to a limp 0.009 detrended. Both numbers go up over the years. That's it. That's the relationship.

So the weekly scoreboard reads: two pairs that technically held up under detrending (one impressively weak, one just two financial aggregates ballooning in tandem), and three glorious trend mirages that imploded the second we asked them to prove themselves. As always, I must legally remind you that these are randomly paired economic series with zero causal story, zero hypothesis, and zero reason to be compared -- spurious correlation isn't a bug here, it's the whole brand. If you walk away from this convinced that Chilean bond flows secretly control nothing in particular, congratulations, you've understood the project perfectly. See you next week.
